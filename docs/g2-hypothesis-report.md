# G2 hypothesis report

Date: 2026-11-01
Branch: `release-curate`
Scope: the 25 labelled rows in `mock_dataset/sample_requests.csv`.

## Verdict in one paragraph

G2 does not fail for one reason, and it does not fail for a reason that
parameter tuning can fix. Two defects were confirmed and are now fixed: the
income projection drifted off the salary payday and capped below the number of
paydays a 90-day window contains. That fix moved four of the five scored fields
(`affordability_status` 14→18, `recommended_payment_method` 14→18,
`payment_plan` 12→17, `earliest_date_for_full_payment` 11→16) while G1 stayed at
0 status upgrades, 0 safe overclaims and 0 unparsed amounts. The blocking field
is `amount_safe_to_pay`, which remains at 3/25. Across roughly 250 scored
configurations — every structural, estimator, horizon, window-start, proration,
month-to-date, event-status and income variant tested — **not one row whose gold
amount is strictly between 0 and the requested amount was ever reproduced**. The
sample gold amounts are therefore not generated by this forecast family, and
20/25 full rows is unreachable by tuning. Reaching it needs the organiser's
reference arithmetic, which the repo does not contain.

## Method

`tools/g2_probe.py` replays the 25 sample requests directly against the kernel,
bypassing the pipeline. Its default knobs reproduce the recorded kernel amounts
**25/25**, so each hypothesis is a knob rather than a slow full run. Variants
patch `code.forecast._monthly_budget_history`, `_monthly_budget_forecast` and
`_income_forecast` inside a context manager and are scored by exact match,
median relative error and MAPE. "Informative" means `0 < gold < requested`; a
row whose gold equals the request is capped and proves nothing about the
arithmetic.

## The blocking finding

Every configuration, including the best-known structural one
(`partial=exclude`, `mtd_scope=recurring_only`, `proration=actual`,
calendar income, cap 3), lands on exactly the same exact-match set:
`request_01`, `request_09`, `request_12`, `request_16` — all rows where the gold
amount is the requested amount or the arithmetic is trivially satisfied. The
informative set is empty everywhere.

The strongest single piece of evidence is the implied day-0 outflow: solving
`gold = balance − floor − X` gives an `X` that is *larger* than our day-0 lump on
almost every row (`request_10` needs 512,055 against our 96,489), but not by a
constant factor (1.05 on `request_03`, 1.09 on `request_04`, 1.06 on
`request_02`). A single missing term, proration rule or estimator cannot produce
that pattern, and the trough diagnostic shows gold's binding day is not always
day 0 — several rows bind on later real events (`request_04` on 2024-06-11
education, `request_02` on 2025-08-08 shopping).

## What was changed

`code/forecast.py`:

- `MAX_INCOME_OCCURRENCES` 2 → 3. A 90-day horizon can contain three monthly
  paydays; capping at two silently dropped the third salary.
- `_projected_income_dates` projects a monthly salary on its calendar day of
  month when the observations cluster on one day, instead of stepping a fixed
  day count. A 15th payday stepped by 30 days becomes the 14th, then the 13th.
- The calendar reading is only used when the median interval is 28–31 days and
  at least 70% of observations fall within three days of the median day.

`tests/test_forecast.py`: the two cap-counting tests now pin three occurrences
(including a third real in-window row that genuinely fills the cap), and a new
test pins the 15th-to-the-15th projection.

## What would actually be needed

1. The organiser's reference implementation, or a labelled row whose gold amount
   is informative and reproducible, to identify the missing term.
2. Failing that, G2's 20/25 target should be re-baselined explicitly rather than
   left failing silently. This report does not change the threshold.

### The decisive constraint

`git log` shows `sample_requests.csv` and its completed gold arrived in the
original challenge dataset commits (`6b07d86`, `ffd788d`, `d675abf`), before any
code in this repository existed; only three commits have ever touched
`code/forecast.py`, and none of them is the generator. The gold is the
organiser's reference output over an implementation this repo does not contain.

Consequently the target is not a function of the features a request exposes:

- a random forest over balance, floor, requested amount, category budgets,
  month-to-date spend, days left, deadline distance, day of month and category
  count generalises to 2/25 rows with a 71% median error;
- a deep tree reproduces 25/25 in sample, which is memorisation, not a rule;
- no category subset, horizon, proration rule, MTD scope, estimator, history
  window or income cadence reproduces even one row where `0 < gold < requested`.

`amount_safe_to_pay` therefore cannot be closed by further search inside this
repository. Reaching G2's 20/25 requires the organiser's reference arithmetic,
extra labelled rows, or an explicit re-baseline of the threshold — a product
decision, not something this report performs.

## Third pass: statistical and model-mining sweep

Scripts: `tools/g2_probe.py` (`actual_excl` proration knob) plus a one-off
`sklearn` sweep. Targets, per row: `D = balance − floor − gold`, `D/full`,
`D/(full − mtd)` and `gold`.

| Model | Target | Leave-one-out result |
|---|---|---|
| OLS | `D/full` | R² −2.4e6 (diverges; 21 rows, 22 collinear features) |
| Ridge (α=1) | `D/full` | R² −0.183, median abs error 0.493 |
| Lasso (α=0.01) | `D/full` | R² −0.315, median abs error 0.557 |
| Decision tree (depth 3) | `D/full` | interpretable splits below |
| Random forest (400) | `D/full` | R² **+0.338**, median abs error **0.166** |
| Gradient boosting | `D/full` | R² +0.128, median abs error 0.145 |
| Random forest | `gold` | 2/25 exact, median relative error 0.714 |

The linear family fails outright — the relation is not linear and the target is
heavy-tailed (`D` CV 2.01). The forest and the tree both concentrate on one
variable: **`full_ratio = monthly budget ÷ balance` carries 74% of the forest's
importance**, then `mtd_ratio` (12%) and `income_cover` (8%).

Interpretable tree on `D/full` (depth 3, min leaf 3):

```
full_ratio <= 0.267            -> 2.338   (budget small vs balance: multi-month deficit)
full_ratio >  0.267
  mtd_ratio <= 0.325
      days_per_month <= 28.5   -> 0.779
      days_per_month >  28.5   -> 0.929
  mtd_ratio >  0.325
      mtd <= 768               -> 0.439
      mtd >  768               -> 0.588
```

That is the shape a 90-day ledger minimum must have: a rich user's safe amount is
a multiple of several months of budget, a user who has already spent most of the
month is protected by roughly the remainder. It is consistent with the existing
architecture — which is why no missing *term* was found.

Unsupervised: `KMeans` (k=2…5) over the 250 real requests splits rows into a
tight group (`D/full` ≈ 0.67 ± 0.20) and a dispersed group (0.94–0.97 ± 0.8);
the 25 labelled rows distribute across those clusters roughly proportionally to
the 250, so the sample is not a biased subpopulation. `PCA` needs four
components for 65% of variance, and its first component loads evenly on balance,
floor, monthly budget and salary — one "size" axis, no separate hidden factor.

### One measurable improvement, still not the rule

`actual_excl` proration — numerator `days_left − 1`, denominator the real days
in the month — beats the shipped rule on both the amount and the date:

| Config | MAPE | earliest matches | informative exact |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| shipped (`current` proration) | 0.613 | 18/25 | 0 |
| `actual` (days_left / days_in_month) | 0.568 | 17/25 | 0 |
| `actual_excl` + exclude partial month + MTD recurring-only | **0.486** | **19/25** | 0 |

`request_02` moves from 12.11M to 13.88M against a 17.23M target under this rule
— the right direction at roughly the right magnitude, but the remaining gap is
not a proration artefact.

### What the sweep rules out

No model reaches exactness out of sample (best: 2/25), the best learner still
carries a 15–17% error on the normalised target, and the residual has no cluster
structure that a single alternative term could explain. The information content
is "the reference is the same kind of ledger as ours, differing in magnitude of
expense and income", not "a term is missing".

### Why the models decide what they decide

SHAP attributions (XGBoost, native `pred_contribs`) rank the drivers as
`full_ratio` 0.405, `mtd_ratio` 0.101, `full` 0.037, `mtd` 0.021, `has_rent`
0.017, `income_cover` 0.013. Partial dependence of `full_ratio` is a step, not a
slope:

```
full_ratio  0.22 -> 2.953      (monthly budget under a quarter of the balance)
            0.31 -> 0.655
            0.40 -> 0.655
            0.50 -> 0.654
            0.68 -> 0.623
```

Two regimes, split near 0.25–0.31. Inside the second regime the tree splits again
on `mtd_ratio` at 0.325 (0.78/0.93 when little of the month is spent, 0.44/0.59
when most of it is). Depth-3 leaves land on 0.439, 0.588, 0.779, 0.929 and 2.338,
which is why 19 of 21 rows sit within 0.09 of a leaf value while never equalling
gold.

The rows the model gets *over* versus *under* differ most in `mtd_ratio` (0.427
versus 0.323) and `to_deadline` (40 versus 56 days) — the model is guessing at how
much of the current month is committed, exactly the quantity that decides the
first trough.

XGBoost reproduces all 21 rows to three decimals **in sample**, which is
memorisation and confirms the earlier point: with 21 rows and 26 encoded
features, any flexible learner can fit the sample and none can generalise.

### Conclusion of the mining

The reference behaves like a ledger with two regimes keyed on capacity
(monthly budget relative to balance) and one on progress through the month
(month-to-date spend). Both are quantities the shipped kernel already models.
Nothing the zoo extracted points to a missing term; what remains unrecovered is
the *magnitude* of the reference's expense and income series, which the sample
does not determine.

## Fifth pass: inside the 21/21 fit

The overfitted ensemble is itself evidence, so its geometry was mined directly:
split gain, chosen thresholds, the first trees in full, leaf co-occurrence
proximity across all 300 trees, and a tree distilled from the ensemble's output.

**Gain.** The ensemble spends its loss budget almost entirely on one split:
`full_ratio < 0.2532` has gain 6.87 while the next best split has 0.45. Nine
distinct `full_ratio` thresholds appear (0.242, 0.2532, 0.2806, 0.3098, 0.3105,
0.4671, 0.49, 0.6218, 0.6386) and seven on `mtd_ratio`; every one is an adjacent
pair of observed values, not a rounded rule. 1,243 splits across 300 trees is
what separating 21 individual points costs.

**Proximity.** Landing in the same leaf across all 300 trees, **all 21 rows are
singletons at a 0.80 threshold**. No two requests are alike enough for the model
to generalise between them — the 21/21 is per-row partitioning, and it is the
cleanest possible demonstration that the sample cannot support a learned rule.

**Distilled surrogate** (a depth-4 tree fitted to the ensemble's own output)
recovers the coarse function the ensemble approximates:

```
full_ratio <= 0.248                  -> 2.9 / 3.1   (only request_05 and request_10)
full_ratio >  0.248
  mtd_ratio <= 0.325
      mtd_ratio <= 0.110             -> 0.86 / 0.98  (split on balance)
      mtd_ratio >  0.110             -> 0.72 / 0.75  (split on day of month)
  mtd_ratio >  0.325
      mtd <= 768                     -> 0.43 / 0.47  (split on income cover)
      mtd >  768                     -> 0.52 / 0.70  (split on month-to-date spend)
```

**The formula behind it.** Sorting the 19 normal rows by `mtd_ratio` gives
`D/full ≈ 1 − mtd_ratio` with r = +0.822 and a median absolute error of 0.068 —
by far the closest closed form found anywhere in this investigation. The residual
correlates most with `to_deadline` (+0.292), i.e. a second-order horizon term, and
with nothing else above +0.20.

**Why our figure is always the larger one.** Decomposing
`D_ours − D_gold` over all 21 rows: our ledger drops further than the reference's
on **every** row, by 1.02× to 1.63×. On the thirteen rows with no real in-window
debit at all the excess is therefore purely forecast magnitude. The largest
ratios (1.63 on `request_08`, 1.43 on `request_14`, 1.37 on `request_13`) are the
rows whose ledger trough lands late — 89, 14 and 61 days out — which is the
income side, not the expense side: `request_08`'s latest salary is a pay cut to
782.57 against a 1,422.85 median, so projecting the latest amount drives its
ledger into a slow decline.

That identifies one concrete, defensible improvement — project salary from the
median rather than the latest observation — but it does not change the match
count, so it is recorded here rather than shipped on speculation.
